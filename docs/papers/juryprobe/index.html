JuryProbe: An Empirical Consensus-Risk Diagnostic for Routing Reference-Free Factuality Judge Panels to Grounded Verification

Tianxin Zhou · Ruixi Lin

TMLR 2026arXiv:2608.20607

Abstract

Author-written abstract from arXiv:2608.20607v2.

Panels of inexpensive LLM judges increasingly make accept-or-escalate decisions. In factuality settings, accepting a claim because several reference-free judges agree can create a hidden risk: agreement may reflect shared false-negative blind spots rather than independent evidence. We introduce JuryProbe, an empirical consensus-risk diagnostic for reference-free factuality judge panels, paired with a calibration-based routing policy. JuryProbe estimates consensus risk from a labeled calibration probe using false-negative-only (FN-only) judge correlation and false-consensus lift; when flagged high-risk, reference-free majority accepts are routed to the same judges with trusted references. On audited FEVER corruptions, reference-free panels show correlated false negatives (FN-only correlations 0.402 and 0.368; lifts 3.13x and 18.13x), while unanimous false consensus drops to zero under a trusted-reference best-case diagnostic on both minimal-pair and non-minimal-pair evidence. In flagged settings, the routed policy is by construction equivalent to grounding every reference-free majority accept (verified in 34/34 splits): improvement comes from accept-conditioned grounding, while the diagnostic determines whether to activate it. A fixed, pre-specified rule flags 8-10 of 10 splits across synthetic, benchmark-authored, and scientific families and 0 of 10 on a negative control, where standing down avoids 28% of reference acquisitions at a 0.004 increase in false accepts. False-accept reduction persists under weak BM25 retrieval at substantial coverage cost, while stale stand-down labels require periodic recalibration. JuryProbe provides no formal risk guarantee and does not establish reliable stand-down on natural panels; its supported contribution is an empirical diagnostic of high-risk panel error dependence.

Research question

JuryProbe tests whether agreement among reference-free factuality judges hides shared errors, then uses calibration evidence to decide when accepted claims need grounded verification.

Method & reported findings

  • A labeled calibration probe measures correlated missed corruptions and excess unanimous false acceptance. When panel-level risk is flagged, every reference-free majority acceptance is rechecked by the same judges with references. Source: v2 §3.2, v2 §3.4
  • On Number and Entity corruptions, false-negative correlations over all corrupted claims were 0.402 and 0.368. False-consensus lifts on GPT-4o-detectable subsets were 3.13× and 18.13×, respectively. Source: v2 Table 1
  • All 34 flagged splits matched the Ground-All-Accepts policy. On a constructed negative control, stand-down avoided reference acquisition for about 28% of deployment claims, with a 0.004 absolute false-accept-rate increase versus Ground-All-Accepts. Source: v2 §5.3, v2 Table 4

Scope & limitations

  • The diagnostic offers no formal risk guarantee and does not establish reliable stand-down for natural panels.
  • Trusted-reference experiments are best-case diagnostics. Weak retrieval reduces true-accept coverage, and distribution shifts require recalibration.

Researcher questions

Are multiple agreeing LLM judges reliable evidence of factual correctness?

Not automatically. Different reference-free judges can miss the same factual corruption, so unanimous agreement can be wrong. JuryProbe measures shared false-negative errors on labeled calibration claims using false-negative correlation and false-consensus lift. It assesses a panel’s risk regime, rather than certifying each new claim. Source: v2 §3.1, v2 §3.2

What is false unanimous consensus, and how does JuryProbe measure it?

False unanimous consensus occurs when every judge accepts a corrupted claim. False-consensus lift compares the observed unanimous false-accept rate with the rate expected if the judges’ errors were independent. A lift above one indicates excess joint failure on that evaluation subset. Table 1 reports false-negative correlations over all corrupted claims separately from lift on the GPT-4o-detectable subset; these quantities have different sample scopes. Source: v2 §3.2, v2 Table 1

How does JuryProbe differ from disagreement-based escalation?

Disagreement routing cannot catch a corrupted claim accepted unanimously. When calibration flags a panel, JuryProbe rechecks every majority acceptance with references, including unanimous accepts. An unflagged panel is not certified safe. Source: v2 §2.4, v2 §3.4

Does evidence grounding eliminate correlated errors in LLM judges?

No. No unanimous false consensus was observed with trusted references in the tested Number/Entity FEVER subsets and SciFact diagnostic, but this does not establish error independence or a universal guarantee. On SciFact, pairwise false-negative correlation remained positive with rationale or full-abstract evidence, even though no unanimous false consensus was observed. Grounding and independent errors are different claims. Source: v2 Table 2, v2 §5.2, v2 Table 10

What changes when references come from weak retrieval?

Evidence quality matters. In the BM25 retrieval stress test, grounding reduced false accepts but also reduced acceptance of clean claims. This is a coverage tradeoff, not evidence that weak retrieval outperforms trusted references. Trusted-reference results are best-case diagnostics, rather than a guarantee for a deployed retrieval system. Source: v2 §5.5, v2 Table 15, v2 Table 16

Can an unflagged judge panel safely skip verification?

JuryProbe provides no formal risk guarantee and does not establish reliable stand-down for natural panels. Its routing decision comes from a labeled calibration probe, not a per-claim safety score. When judges or the claim distribution change, stale calibration can miss renewed consensus risk; the paper studies periodic recalibration as a response. Source: v2 §3.4, v2 §6.1, v2 §6.2
