JuryProbe · Research paper

JuryProbe: An Empirical Consensus-Risk Diagnostic for Routing Reference-Free Factuality Judge Panels to Grounded Verification

Tianxin Zhou · Ruixi Lin

TMLR 2026arXiv:2608.20607

Research question

JuryProbe tests whether agreement among reference-free factuality judges hides shared errors, then uses calibration evidence to decide when accepted claims need grounded verification.

Method & reported findings

  • A labeled calibration probe measures correlated missed corruptions and excess unanimous false acceptance. When panel-level risk is flagged, every reference-free majority acceptance is rechecked by the same judges with references.
  • On Number and Entity corruptions, false-negative correlations over all corrupted claims were 0.402 and 0.368. False-consensus lifts on GPT-4o-detectable subsets were 3.13× and 18.13×, respectively.
  • All 34 flagged splits matched the Ground-All-Accepts policy. On a constructed negative control, stand-down avoided reference acquisition for about 28% of deployment claims, with a 0.004 absolute false-accept-rate increase versus Ground-All-Accepts.

Scope & limitations

  • The diagnostic offers no formal risk guarantee and does not establish reliable stand-down for natural panels.
  • Trusted-reference experiments are best-case diagnostics. Weak retrieval reduces true-accept coverage, and distribution shifts require recalibration.
